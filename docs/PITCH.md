# PITCH — 3 minutes

> Status: living draft — not final; update as decisions change. docs/CASE.md (official task) wins on conflict, then SPEC, then PRD. Source (RU): docs/research/raw/deep-research-output.ru.md

Translated from the `docs/PITCH.md` block of the deep-research output. This is a spoken 3-minute script.

**Note:** the live pitch to the jury may be delivered in Russian; this English version is the reference script for translation, review, and reuse.

**0:00–0:20 — The problem.** Classic voice robots pick a scenario with an encoder-based classifier. It breaks on topic switches, on the boundary between scenarios, and on Russian↔Kazakh switching inside a single phrase. The result: a transfer to a human operator, or a lost customer.

**0:20–0:50 — Bagyt's idea.** We replaced the classifier with an LLM routing layer. Embeddings produce a short list of candidates out of 40 scenarios, and the decision — with reasoning — is made by a generative model that outputs strict JSON. This isn't a black box: you can see the candidates, the prompt, and why a given scenario was chosen.

**0:50–1:40 — Live demo.** I speak in Russian — the robot answers. Official example: "оплатил, деньги списались, заказ не подтвердился… и адрес поменять" (I paid, the money was deducted, the order wasn't confirmed... and I need to change the address) — the robot holds a topic stack, resolves the payment first, then returns to the address. An utterance in Kazakh — it answers in Kazakh. A borderline utterance — the robot asks a clarifying question instead of guessing.

**1:40–2:20 — Trace and supervisor.** After every utterance: the scenario, reasoning, alternatives, per-stage latency. The supervisor sees accuracy on `dev_utterances`, and where the robot hesitated or got it wrong.

**2:20–2:50 — Technology and honesty.** RU/KZ and code-switching go through geko (Seta/Tokay, WER 8.71% vs. 44.95% for Whisper) and ElevenLabs. We measure speed openly; where we're not confident, we ask a clarifying question or hand off to an operator with context.

**2:50–3:00 — What's next.** Speculative routing, a phone channel, a no-code catalog editor. Single-command startup, reproducible.

## 8 hard jury questions + answers
1. **"How is this not an intent classifier?"** — Embeddings only shortlist candidates (retrieval). The decision and reasoning are generated by the LLM as commands/JSON (Rasa CALM-style approach). There is no classifier at the point of decision.
2. **"Didn't you hardcode answers for the jury's 10 utterances?"** — No. There are no utterances in the code; the router reads `scenarios.json` dynamically, and embeddings are built from it at startup. Any utterances can be substituted.
3. **"Do you hit 500ms?"** — That's a target for bonus points, not a hard requirement. Honestly: a single cloud LLM's TTFT alone is ~0.45–0.6s, so a full route takes ~500–900ms; we show real measurements and the speculative path that hides the latency by the end of the utterance.
4. **"Does Kazakh actually work, or is it just for show?"** — geko Seta listens to KZ/RU in a single vocabulary with switching mid-word (WER 8.71%); two Kazakh utterances and a mixed one are part of the demo.
5. **"What happens when the router gets it wrong?"** — Low confidence → a clarifying question or handoff to an operator with context. AI is not the sole source of truth.
6. **"Does this scale to 40+ scenarios?"** — The shortlist keeps the prompt short regardless of the number of scenarios; adding a scenario means adding a record to `scenarios.json` — no code changes needed.
7. **"Why not train your own model?"** — Per the task spec it provides no advantage, and encoder-based classifiers are forbidden; our focus is on LLM routing quality, not on training a model.
8. **"What about irreversible actions?"** — The robot doesn't perform them without the client's explicit confirmation; the trace shows what is proposed and why.
